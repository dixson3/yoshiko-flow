plan-049-james-dixson-725bc0 has landed and closes this. Two mechanisms, both shipped:

**1. Self-exclusion (primary).** `--exclude <glob>`, repeatable, on **both**
`_shared/doc_lint.py` and `_shared/plan_extract.py`. A plan's corpus measurements exclude the
plan itself. It is applied even under `doc_lint`'s `--no-exclude` positive control, because a
schema's own carve-out and a caller's self-exclusion are different kinds of thing — a positive
control that silently re-admitted the measuring plan would reintroduce exactly the
self-reference the flag removes. The excluded set is **reported**, never silently dropped.

**2. One narrow in-flight rule.** A `stale-measured-literal` check reporting a bare number
written beside a corpus-measurement noun. Scoped hard, and each rule is earned:

- `bundle_status != complete` — a finished plan's measurement is a **historical record** and is
  supposed to be frozen;
- skips `findings/` and `reviews/` — those are point-in-time records by construction;
- severity `W` with a **check-level** `promote = false`, so a hint can never hard-fail intake
  while `required-sections` keeps promoting.

**Measured both ways:** it fires on **zero** of the 49 finished corpus plans and on **3**
findings in the same bundle forced in-flight — so the silence is scoping, not a dead rule. The
naive form was measured at **41/41 with 39 correct-behaviour false positives** (EXP-005).

**The blind spot is stated, in all three places a reader can meet the rule** (the finding text,
the schema declaration, the engine docstring): it is **denominator-only**. It finds a stale
*count*; it cannot find a stale *claim about* a count. "the populations overlap at 144 of 1340"
drifts silently when 1340 moves, and "roughly a third" carries no literal at all. A
numerator-drift instance passes green.
